All the World’s a Stage

William Shakespeare · As You Like It · C1 video lesson

C1 · Literature & language

Life as a performance

Listen to Shakespeare describe seven stages of life. Learn the meaning of older words, follow the main images, and discuss how people change as they grow older.

Before watching

  1. What different roles do you have at home, at school or at work?
  2. What do children and older people sometimes have in common?
  3. Which stage of life do you think is the most difficult? Why?
  4. How can clothes show a person’s age or job?
  5. What might the phrase “life is a play” mean?

This speech comes from As You Like It. A metaphor describes one thing as another. Here, the world becomes a stage and people become actors.

Listening focus

Listen for these five stages: infant, schoolboy, lover, soldier and justice (a judge). What is each person doing? Which description is funny?

Comprehension 1

Answer in your own words, then select a question to see a suggested answer.

What does the speaker compare the world to?

He compares it to a stage. Men and women are like actors who play different roles.

What do “entrances” and “exits” suggest about life?

An entrance suggests birth and an exit suggests death.

What is the infant doing?

The infant is crying and being sick in the nurse’s arms.

Why is the schoolboy compared to a snail?

He moves very slowly because he does not want to go to school.

What is the lover doing, and how does he feel?

He is sighing and writing a sad song for the woman he loves. His feelings seem very strong.

Why is reputation compared to a bubble?

A bubble can disappear quickly. The image suggests that fame is fragile, even though the soldier risks his life for it.

How is the justice different from the soldier?

The soldier is quick to fight and wants honour. The justice looks serious and well fed, and gives advice using familiar sayings.

Listening focus

Listen for changes in the old man’s body and voice. Which word is repeated in the final line?

Comprehension 2

Answer in your own words, then select a question to see a suggested answer.

What has happened to the old man’s legs?

They have become thinner, so the stockings he kept from his youth are now too wide.

How has his voice changed?

His deep adult voice has become high and thin, like a child’s.

What does “second childishness” suggest?

The person becomes dependent on others again, as they were in early childhood.

What does “sans” mean?

It means “without.”

What effect does repeating “sans” have?

The repeated word adds one loss after another and makes the final line sound powerful and final.

Which image connects the ending to the beginning?

“Last scene of all” returns to the idea that life is a play.

“Modern instances” means contemporary examples. “Capon lin’d” presents the justice as well fed. These meanings belong to the context of the speech.

Use the vocabulary

“creeping like snail”

Which word describes the lover’s sorrowful song?

woeful

bubble reputation

treble

Which word means “without” in the final line?

Discussion

  1. Which description is easiest to imagine? Explain why.
  2. Do you find the speech mainly funny, sad or both?
  3. Is the soldier right to risk his life for reputation?
  4. How is life today different from the life described in the speech?
  5. Would you divide life into the same seven stages?
  6. What might an eighth stage look like?

Useful language

The image makes me think of… / Compared with…, … / I find this stage sad because… / In my opinion…, although…

Performance and speaking

Student A Choose one stage and describe the person’s clothes, movement and feelings.

Student B Guess the stage and explain which words helped you. Then swap roles.

Read aloud

Choose four to six lines. Decide which words to stress and where to pause. Read them once with a serious tone and once with a humorous tone. Discuss which version you prefer.

Writing

Write 140–180 words comparing two stages of life. Refer to two details from the speech. Use one comparative form and one sentence with too or enough.
